Data & methodology.

ARES loads a measured Jezero CTX optical mosaic and digital elevation model (DEM) crop by default. A 30 × 22.5 km measured overview supports geographic exploration; routes remain limited to the validated 3.2 × 2.4 km study crop.

INDEPENDENT RESEARCH PROTOTYPE · NOT CERTIFIED EVA NAVIGATIONARES is an independent research prototype developed for NASA Space Apps Challenge. NASA does not endorse this project.

What is real in ARES?

Measured CTX study crop

Jezero optical mosaic and DEM data are processed into aligned WebP layers and a 80 × 60 height grid at 40 m cell spacing. Map coverage: projected E 4,586,000–4,589,200 m and N 1,091,000–1,093,400 m. Original optical sampling is 6 m/pixel; DEM sampling is 20 m/pixel.

Expanded measured overview

The same registered CTX optical and DEM GeoTIFFs provide a 30 × 22.5 km context view. Pan, zoom and projected-coordinate inspection work throughout the overview. The outlined crop is the only area with validated route planning. The overview does not imply routing outside it.

Route computation

Eight-neighbour A* computes shortest grid distance or a terrain-preferred path with an illustrative slope-cost weighting. The displayed maximum slope is between adjacent 40 m sampled grid cells, not a maximum physical surface slope.

Separate synthetic demonstration

The optional synthetic demo mode uses procedurally generated height values, imagery, hazard zones and thermal colouring. No measured CTX thermal or verified hazard layer is provided.

Known scientific limits

ARES does not resolve rocks or sand at EVA scale, certify traversability, model life-support constraints, or calculate local Mars radiation exposure. User-selected points and example waypoints are not authenticated named geological targets. Outside-crop targets are not routed.

Attribution and product identity

CTX is an instrument aboard NASA's Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter. USGS Astrogeology publishes the processed Mars 2020 map products used here. NASA-derived research data does not make ARES a NASA-developed or NASA-endorsed product. ARES implements its own route model, editor and interface. No NASA insignia or mission badge is used.
