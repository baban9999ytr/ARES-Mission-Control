## Processing and measured coverage

`tools/import_mars_rasters.py` reproducibly samples the 3.2 × 2.4 km study crop, projected E 4,586,000–4,589,200 m and N 1,091,000–1,093,400 m. The 80 × 60 route grid has 40 m square cells. The script rejects mismatched CRS, invalid bounds and missing DEM samples. It builds aligned optical/elevation WebP layers and `public/data/mission-pack.json`. The original source TIFFs stay outside the static deployment.

`tools/build_ctx_overview.py` reproducibly samples a 30 × 22.5 km overview, E 4,574,000–4,604,000 m and N 1,080,000–1,102,500 m, from the same registered GeoTIFFs. It rejects mismatched CRS, out-of-bounds sampling and any nodata pixels. Its 1,600 × 1,200 display rasters are visual products, with approximately 18.75 m output pixel spacing; that display sampling is not new independent DEM resolution. The overview is context-only outside the selected routeable region. `public/data/ctx-overview.json` records the exact extent, source URLs and display sampling.

Phase 9.1 adds a separately generated 6.4 × 4.8 km route sector, E 4,584,400–4,590,800 m and N 1,089,800–1,094,600 m. It uses the same CTX rasters, ESRI:103885 CRS and 40 m grid spacing. `tools/import_mars_rasters.py` generated the 160 × 120 DEM grid and aligned WebP layers; `tools/audit_expanded_rasters.py` independently reprojects both source rasters and compares all 19,200 stored heights (maximum observed float32 difference 0.0004883 m). Every cell in this selected sector has valid optical and DEM input. The null height representation remains the route exclusion mask for other regions. `public/data/ctx-expanded-pack.json` preserves source URLs, CRS, bounds, vertical reference and quality counts. The original crop remains available and is used for the 30 authored exercises. The overview outlines whichever of these two route regions is selected.

The overview optical and elevation WebPs total roughly 0.52 MB; the expanded sector's two WebPs total about 0.14 MB and its numerical pack is about 0.19 MB. The raw CTX GeoTIFFs total roughly 46 MB and are not deployed. Two local high-resolution Jezero pairs use a different Mars CRS and total several gigabytes. They have not been merged into the app or routed. Additional regions should become separate measured packs with their own CRS, bounds, nodata checks, tile pyramids, validation and explicit route eligibility. No full-planet routing is claimed.

## Route and segment methodology

ARES implements eight-neighbour A* with a binary min-heap on the sampled grid. Orthogonal centre-to-centre steps are 40 m, diagonals are 40√2 ≈ 56.57 m. Shortest mode minimizes grid distance. Terrain-preferred mode applies the illustrative cost `length × (1 + 4 × (slope / 20°)²)`; it does not certify traversability. A straight-line Euclidean heuristic remains admissible because every edge costs at least its horizontal length. Each multi-stop leg is planned independently in fixed order. Segment elevation is read from the DEM sample at each endpoint cell centre. Reported segment mean is the arithmetic mean of these two sampled endpoints. Change is end minus start. Approximate step slope is atan(|change| / horizontal step length) in degrees. Total ascent sums positive endpoint differences. No interpolation across the full physical segment is claimed. Rocks, sand, lighting, communications, suit limits and local surface radiation are not assessed. [The 30-mission comparison audit](docs/phase9/PHASE_9_1_ROUTE_AUDIT.md) records route similarities and differences.

## Context feeds and illustrative data

[NASA Mars Trek](https://trek.nasa.gov/mars/) and the [Perseverance location map](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/location-map/) are research context, not ARES routing inputs. The optional [NASA CCMC DONKI](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/tools/DONKI/) feed reports space-weather events; it does not measure local Mars surface radiation. The five permanent Jezero landmarks have fixed ESRI:103885 metre coordinates and are illustrative study locations, not real human installations, mineral discoveries or authenticated named geology. They remain visible independently of mission state unless the user hides the landmark layer. The separate synthetic demo uses generated imagery, heights, thermal-style colors, hazard circles and synthetic map landmarks with no Mars georeference.

USGS asks reusers to [credit its publications and authors](https://www.usgs.gov/information-policies-and-instructions/acknowledging-or-crediting-usgs).
